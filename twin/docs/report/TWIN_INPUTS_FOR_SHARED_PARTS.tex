\section*{Twin inputs for the shared parts}
The Twin team's contribution to \textbf{Part 0} (triplet overview and integration) and \textbf{Part 4} (Test Book). The Guardian and Brain columns state what each partner layer is designed to do, from its code; what we actually observed in the joint runs is in the last section, \emph{Observed in the joint runs}.

\subsection*{Part 0 input: one scenario traced through the three layers}
\textbf{Port flood in La Goulette (scenario T3).} A storm triples the inflow to the drainage channel in zone 2. The Twin's physics raises the real water level at floats \texttt{z2-wl1} and \texttt{z2-wl2}, and both keep reporting ten times per second on Kafka, each reading signed with its own Ed25519 key (for example \texttt{"device\_id":"z2-wl1"}, \texttt{"value":6.92}, \texttt{"unit":"meters"}, \texttt{"status":"ok"}, plus its signature). The Guardian verifies every signature with the public key it fetched from the Twin over MCP (\texttt{get\_device\_key}), checks the reading against its rules and models, and passes it on as trusted. The Brain sees the level climbing toward the 8\,m danger line on two agreeing, trusted sensors and calls the Twin's MCP tool \texttt{set\_valve \{"device\_id":"z2-wl1","target\_state":"open"\}}. The Twin applies the command to the physics: the drain opens, the level peaks around 7.3\,m and never crosses 8\,m. The ground-truth ledger records the storm, the command (caller \texttt{brain}, executed) and the real level, so the run log \texttt{logs/t3-*.log} proves the outcome independently of what any layer claims.

\textbf{What the Twin contributes to the triplet:} the stream (Kafka, signed, $\le$ 300\,B, keyed by device), the command surface (16 MCP tools: queries, actuation, containment), the attack engine that gives the Guardian something real to catch, and the ground truth the jury can check afterwards.

\textbf{Shared decisions the Twin relies on:} Kafka with SASL on the Guardian's three-broker cluster (the Twin writes \texttt{telemetry-ingest} as user \texttt{twin}); MCP over HTTP on port 8000 with one bearer token per layer; Ed25519 over the sorted compact JSON without \texttt{sig}; device public keys come only from \texttt{get\_device\_key}; containment (isolate, quarantine) is executed by the Twin at the Guardian's request.

\subsection*{Part 4 input: Twin scenarios}
Every scenario starts with one command and writes \texttt{logs/tN-<timestamp>.log}:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=0pt,topsep=1pt]
  \item \texttt{export RED\_TEAM\_TOKEN=\$(docker exec twin\_layer cat /app/data/red\_team.token)}
  \item \texttt{python3 scenarios/run\_all.py -{}-with-mocks} (all 11, about 5 minutes at \texttt{SIM\_SPEED=20}), or one at a time, e.g.\ \texttt{python3 scenarios/t6\_fake\_port\_flood.py -{}-with-mocks}
\end{itemize}
\texttt{-{}-with-mocks} uses our stand-in Guardian and Brain; \texttt{-{}-partners} runs the same trigger against the real partner layers and sends no actuation or containment itself.

\textbf{Coverage of the interactions required by the rules.} Twin $\to$ Brain (real events): T2 accident, T3 port flood, T4 fire, T5 grid overload. Twin $\to$ Guardian (healthy vs unhealthy data): T1 baseline, T7 stuck sensor, T8 rogue device, T9 stolen key, T10 link tampering. Brain $\leftrightarrow$ Guardian (trust changes decisions): T6 fake port flood, T9 stolen key. All three: T11 storm with a lying actuator.
